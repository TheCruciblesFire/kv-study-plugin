# Devotional editorial review

This reference owns editorial quality only. Use specialist Skills for source disclosure, passage-faithfulness classification, quotation/permission checks, and multi-asset scope.

1. Confirm requested mode and quantity. A plan must remain a plan; a full week must contain five finished days; a single day must not expand into a week.
2. Compare the actual output with the selected reading-plan entries or supplied handoff. Preserve locked readings, titles, goal, teaching, rhythm, and cautions. Flag meaningful source conflicts rather than silently editing the plan.
3. Check whether each day has a distinct role, text-dependent burden, proportionate application, and a natural reflection/prayer/response progression.
4. Assess continuity only against material actually supplied. Keep Inherited Truth, New Contribution, and Forward Movement in an optional editor-facing note, never as unexplained labels inside public prose.
5. Review body length under the active requirement. When tightening, remove repetition before text-rooted substance. Audit reports identify the body count (measured or estimated), range status, and paragraphs best suited for trimming.
6. Check connected prose, repetitive hooks, imagery, emotional movement, stock contrast patterns, cliches, unnecessary em dashes, and repeated prayer structures.
7. Confirm each Finish This Sentence stem is unique, incomplete, and grows from that day's developed application. Avoid identical openings across consecutive days.
8. Ensure alternate titles remain alternatives, social summaries include the title and introduce no new ideas, and the final response contains no unrequested downstream package.

When an audit is requested, return concise findings by severity, what should be preserved, and concrete revision recommendations. When revision is requested, deliver the revised devotional first and only necessary notes. Do not claim cross-series comparison, source verification, or theological review beyond the work actually performed.
