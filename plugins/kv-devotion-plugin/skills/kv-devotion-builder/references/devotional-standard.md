# Devotional standard

## Format and scope

A full week normally contains its number/title when supplied, Main Goal, Core Teaching, Weekly Introduction, all five days, two alternate titles per day, and brief two-sentence social summaries per day. Alternatives do not replace locked titles or fixed section labels. Social summaries carry the devotional title and add no teaching. Omit extras when the user narrows scope. Do not invent a week number or claim a newly written goal was already approved.

A single day contains Title, Today's Reading, Key Verse, Devotional, Reflection, Prayer, and Finish This Sentence. Include available week title, Main Goal, and Core Teaching as context when the day belongs to a supplied plan. Do not generate other days.

Use a key verse from the assigned reading and preserve the full reading's context. Prefer one exact, verified verse with reference and translation label; do not substitute remembered wording from another translation. If verification is unavailable, use a clearly labeled reference or paraphrase and disclose the limit. Formatting and link verification belong to the Scripture Skill.

Today's Reading uses readable Scripture references. Preserve the user's supplied or verified BLB links; when links are required, verify the exact destination. Do not fabricate a working URL. Missing link verification must not prevent useful devotional drafting.

## Length

Current user instructions and active Project specifications take precedence over these inherited defaults:

| Component | Default target | Soft ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Devotional body, excluding other sections | 350-500 words | 600 |
| Complete day | 600-700 words | 1,000 |
| Weekly introduction | 175-300 words | 350 |
| Prayer | 75-100 words | Respect complete-day scope |
| Reflection | Two questions, or one short paragraph plus 1-2 questions | Keep proportionate |

These are targets, not quotas. Never pad a short faithful day merely to reach a complete-day total. A Project's 250-275-word body target overrides the general body default and calls for proportionate supporting sections. Count body words separately from headings, reading, verse, reflection, prayer, and prompt. Report measured counts when available; label estimates as estimates.

## Five-day movement

| Day | Role | Guiding question | Writing emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Story | What is God doing, and what develops in the biblical story? | Establish the narrative or literary setting; application remains secondary. |
| 2 | The Worldview | What reality about God, humanity, creation, covenant, worship, or the nations does the text reveal? | Let the text reshape assumptions. |
| 3 | The Heart | What desire, fear, loyalty, misconception, or false story does the text expose? | Invite specific, honest inward response. |
| 4 | The Way | What kind of people does this passage form, and how is faithful life embodied in Christ? | Root concrete obedience in grace and identity; avoid moralism. |
| 5 | Worship & Response | What response most naturally completes the week's journey? | Lead into praise, thanksgiving, confession, lament, surrender, remembrance, hope, intercession, or obedience as warranted. |

Honor genre. Not every psalm or epistle describes a narrative change. Explain its place in God's story without manufacturing a plot. Day 4 remains Christ-centered; no forced Christological symbolism. Do not prematurely resolve tension or force every Day 5 into emotional triumph.

## Voice and variety

Use warm, reverent, adult-readable, narrative prose and natural contractions. Keep paragraphs connected. Short standalone sentences need a clear rhetorical purpose. Avoid runs of isolated ordinary sentences, stock opening hooks, repeated contrast formulas, filler, cliches, and identical emotional arcs. Vary imagery, application, sentence structure, responsible Christological connections, and forms of worship. The no-em-dash rule includes headings and citation separators outside exact Scripture quotations; use parentheses for a verse citation. Preserve punctuation inside an exact supplied quotation.

Keep the body devotional rather than a compressed sermon, commentary, lesson, or academic essay. Preserve the passage's distinctive details so the piece could not be transferred to an unrelated text by changing the citation.

## Finish This Sentence

End each day exactly in this form:

**Finish This Sentence:**

"[A unique passage-shaped sentence stem] __________________."

Make the stem brief, concrete, pastoral, conversational, and connected to an application already developed. Vary its opening across days and available recent weeks. It is an incomplete sentence, not a second question or a generic engagement device. Do not introduce a new claim here. Inviting comments is appropriate when the user's delivery context calls for it.
